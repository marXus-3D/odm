Why eight connections are faster

Hosts cap each socket, not each person. Eight sockets get eight times the cap.

TCP shares a bottleneck per flow. Eight flows claim eight shares, and each ramps up on its own, which matters most on long high-bandwidth paths.

The split is decided late. An idle connection halves the largest remaining range and takes the tail, so a slow socket owns less and less of the file.

Every range writes into the same preallocated file at its own offset. Finished ranges are recorded as they land, which is why the paused download above knows the exact byte it will resume from.
